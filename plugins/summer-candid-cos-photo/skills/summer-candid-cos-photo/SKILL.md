# Summer Candid Cos Photo

Create one believable photograph from a private summer memory: the people were already enjoying the moment, and a companion happened to press the shutter. Keep the result intimate and spontaneous rather than staged, commercial, or voyeuristic.

## Generate the image

Use the available image-generation or image-editing tool. Generate the image directly when the request is sufficiently specified; do not stop to rewrite the request as a prompt unless the user explicitly asks for a prompt.

Produce exactly one standalone image per invocation:

- one frame;
- one location;
- one time;
- one continuous event.

Allow any sensible number of people inside that one photograph. Never turn multiple subjects into a collage, lineup sheet, grid, contact sheet, split screen, storyboard, montage, diptych, triptych, film strip, magazine layout, or nested set of photographs.

If the user requests a batch, several views, or several moments, create only the first requested image in the current invocation and continue one image at a time on follow-up. Do not combine the requested views into one canvas.

## Resolve the request

Honor the user's explicit subject count, identities, characters, clothing, action, location, camera angle, crop, aspect ratio, and photographic treatment. Infer unspecified details without asking unless the missing choice would materially change the result.

Use these defaults when the user leaves them open:

- one clearly adult woman, age 21–30;
- recognizable real-life photography rather than illustration or CGI;
- realistic, wearable swimwear with full coverage of nipples and genitals;
- a relaxed friend/partner travel-companion viewpoint;
- a 28–40 mm lens at conversational distance;
- a lived-in beach, pool, lakeside, or waterside setting;
- one mid-action interaction rather than a formal pose;
- natural summer light and subtle consumer-camera imperfection.

Read [references/visual-system.md](references/visual-system.md) when the request leaves several creative variables unspecified or involves multi-person staging, cosplay translation, complex reference roles, or a specific analog-camera treatment.

## Enforce adult depiction

Depict every swimwear or beachwear subject as an unambiguously adult person aged 21–30, with adult facial features and body proportions. Do not depict children, teenagers, school-age coding, childlike anatomy, or infantilized styling.

When a requested fictional character is underage, age-ambiguous, or visually childlike, create an explicitly adult 21–30-year-old cosplayer reinterpretation. Preserve only the character's recognizable visual DNA and personality; never preserve a childlike body or age presentation.

Keep swimwear non-explicit and securely covering nipples, areolae, and genitals. Translucent, wet, or lightweight fabric may affect texture and highlights but must retain opaque lining and coverage where needed.

## Use reference images by role

Determine what each supplied image controls. Treat an image only as an identity, face, hairstyle, makeup, outfit, accessory, pose, composition, camera, lighting, location, or photographic-style reference unless the user explicitly assigns multiple roles.

- For an identity reference, preserve facial geometry, proportions, hairline, and recognizability while allowing natural changes in expression, wetness, lighting, makeup, and hairstyle state.
- For a pose reference, preserve weight distribution, shoulder/hip relationship, limb direction, head angle, and gaze, then loosen it into a plausible transitional action.
- For a composition reference, preserve camera height, distance, perspective, subject placement, crop, and foreground/background balance without copying its people, clothes, or location.
- For a style reference, preserve exposure, color temperature, contrast, grain, focus behavior, bloom, halation, flash, and framing looseness without copying content.
- For several identity references, maintain a separate identity anchor for each person. Do not blend faces, hairstyles, clothing, or accessories.

If reference roles are genuinely ambiguous and would produce materially different identities or styling, ask one concise question. Otherwise infer the most likely role and proceed.

## Translate character cosplay

Treat fictional characters as high-quality adult real-world cosplayers unless the user explicitly requests the original fictional environment.

Extract the smallest set of traits needed for recognition:

- hair color, cut, bangs, and headwear;
- eye color and makeup language;
- signature palette, emblem, accessory, pattern, or prop;
- characteristic silhouette or structural clothing motif;
- recognizable attitude and behavior.

Default to recognizable real-life cosplay. Translate the character's palette, geometry, motif, and accessory language into believable swimwear rather than putting a full battle costume in water or merely recoloring a generic bikini.

For multiple characters, assign each person an independent identity anchor, character anchor, swimwear silhouette, palette, accessories, and personality. Prevent face swaps, facial blending, hairstyle swaps, palette leakage, outfit leakage, and accessory leakage.

## Stage one real event

Choose one event and make every visible action part of it. Prefer interaction: pulling the photographer toward the water, reacting to a splash, offering a drink, stealing a towel, fixing a friend's hair, blocking the lens, laughing at a companion, running out of a wave, or speaking while turning back.

Show the action in progress through forward weight, shoulder/hip rotation, unfinished gestures, off-balance transitions, wind or water response, and expressions caught between beats. Avoid formal modeling poses, synchronized front-facing groups, evenly spaced lineups, and everyone looking at the camera.

For multiple people, create a reaction chain around the same event. Use asymmetric depth and scale: one person may be near, another mid-ground, and another slightly occluded or softly out of focus, while all remain anatomically distinct.

Allow a small part of the photographer's hand, forearm, knee, or leg only when it proves the point of view and does not compete with the subjects.

## Make it physically believable

Match wetness, hair, cloth, water, and light to what just happened:

- wet hair clumps and adheres naturally to skin or clothing;
- wet fabric darkens, gains weight, and follows gravity without becoming explicit;
- droplets, splashes, reflections, and motion blur follow the action;
- skin retains pores, fine hair, lip and under-eye texture, mild tone variation, natural redness, and restrained sheen;
- joints, fingers, limbs, occlusion, body contact, and muscle tension remain anatomically coherent.

Include at least one water cue: visible water, waves, spray, droplets, wet hair, wet skin, wet paving, ripples, reflections, or an outdoor rinse.

Add only a few lived-in details such as a used towel, damp sandals, sunscreen, a melting drink, a canvas bag, goggles, disturbed sand, or wind-creased fabric.

Choose one primary capture medium. Apply subtle flaws that read as camera behavior—slight crop, imperfect autofocus, soft corners, fine grain, modest flare, highlight bloom, small motion blur, or a slightly tilted horizon. Never imitate a camera UI, viewfinder overlay, date stamp, logo, watermark, or recording indicator unless requested.

## Final check

Before generation, confirm internally:

- every swimwear subject is unmistakably adult;
- the output is one photograph, not a multi-image layout;
- all subjects occupy the same place and instant;
- one central event explains every person's action;
- identity and character traits remain separate and recognizable;
- swimwear is wearable, non-explicit, and character-specific when applicable;
- skin, anatomy, wetness, fabric, light, and water obey physical logic;
- the image reads as a familiar companion's candid summer memory, not an advertisement.

Avoid plastic skin, wax skin, CGI faces, extreme beauty filtering, exaggerated body ratios, duplicated people, duplicated limbs, malformed hands, fused anatomy, generic cosplay faces, commercial swimwear posing, and unrelated simultaneous actions.
