# Visual System

Use this reference to select coherent creative variables without overloading the final image. Choose one option from each relevant category, then express the result as one continuous photographic moment.

## 1. Creative priority

Rank decisions in this order:

1. explicit user instructions and assigned reference roles;
2. adult age clarity and non-explicit coverage;
3. identity preservation;
4. one-image, one-place, one-moment continuity;
5. character recognition and personality;
6. natural interaction and physical logic;
7. photographic texture and atmosphere.

Do not sacrifice identity or continuity merely to add more styling details.

## 2. Subject and character handling

For an original subject, choose a specific but natural presence rather than a generic beauty archetype: cool and quiet, sunny and athletic, mature and relaxed, playful, bookish, casually elegant, or softly cinematic.

For cosplay, choose an intensity:

- Inspired: retain palette, hair language, and attitude; keep the outfit mostly everyday.
- Recognizable real-life cosplay: retain the signature hair, palette, emblem/accessory, silhouette language, and personality; use this by default.
- Full cosplay: retain more original construction, makeup, headwear, and props, while adapting materials and behavior to the summer location.

Use the original fictional environment only when explicitly requested. Otherwise place an adult real-world cosplayer on an ordinary trip.

For multiple people, vary silhouette, neckline, strap structure, color blocking, accessories, distance from camera, gaze, and reaction. Coordinate the group without dressing everyone identically.

## 3. Swimwear translation

Select a wearable base that fits the character and action:

- triangle, halter, bandeau, cross-strap, asymmetric, sport, retro, or high-waist bikini;
- minimal, athletic, square-neck, asymmetric, retro, resort, or architectural one-piece;
- resort separates or beachwear layers;
- designer details such as geometric cuts, restrained metal hardware, structural waist panels, or asymmetric shoulders.

Translate character DNA through structure rather than color alone:

- armor geometry can become seam lines, paneling, or strap architecture;
- capes and flowing garments can become a wrap, overshirt, scarf, or cover-up;
- insignia can become one small clasp, print, charm, or hair accessory;
- elemental motifs can become color gradients, texture, or reflective accents;
- utility costumes can become athletic cuts, belts, waterproof pouches, or sport details.

Keep the design plausible for swimming and movement. Avoid stage-costume bulk, unsupported ornaments, and accidental exposure.

## 4. Event and relationship cues

Select one event with a cause-and-reaction structure:

- subject pulls the photographer into shallow water;
- one friend splashes, another dodges, a third laughs;
- someone offers a cold drink while still speaking;
- one person steals or returns a towel;
- someone helps another fix wet hair;
- the group runs from a breaking wave;
- a subject notices the camera and reaches to block or take it;
- companions sit at the pool edge reacting to the same joke;
- a subject turns back mid-step to wait for the photographer.

Build familiarity through half-finished speech, private smiles, teasing, relaxed eye contact, mild annoyance, shared laughter, or attention directed at another person. Avoid generic seductive staring and identical influencer expressions.

## 5. Locations and lived-in detail

Choose one location:

- private backyard pool, small villa, beach guesthouse, rooftop pool, ordinary hotel pool, tropical courtyard, stone-walled terrace;
- quiet beach, rocky cove, shallow sea, island shore, Mediterranean bay, sunset coastline, wooden pier;
- forest stream, lakeside dock, mountain pool, waterfall downstream, spring-fed pool;
- outdoor beach shower, pool rinse area, stone shower, wooden rinse station, garden hose.

Add no more than a few traces of real use: damp sandals, a wrinkled towel, sunscreen, hat, canvas bag, water bottle, goggles, water toy, beach bag, wet paving, footprints, condensation, a half-open door, or leaves with droplets.

## 6. Capture medium and lens

Choose one medium:

- 35 mm film: organic fine grain, restrained halation, gentle focus falloff, smooth highlight roll-off;
- 1990s compact film: consumer-lens softness, casual autofocus, soft corners, occasional direct flash;
- 2000s CCD: small-sensor texture, limited dynamic range, cooler highlights, direct flash when appropriate;
- disposable camera: fixed-focus feeling, stronger grain, slight color shift, accidental crop;
- early smartphone: casual sharpness, mild motion blur, imperfect HDR, spontaneous crop.

Use 28–40 mm by default. Use 24 mm for intimate close proximity with controlled edge distortion, 50 mm for a calmer portrait distance, and 70 mm only when the scene needs observational compression.

Choose a non-commercial camera height: waterline, pool edge, waist, chest, eye level, seated low angle, subtle low angle, slight overhead, lounge-chair point of view, or seated beach point of view.

## 7. Light, framing, and realism

Choose light consistent with the location and time: morning sun, hard midday sun, leaf-filtered shade, warm afternoon, golden hour, overcast softness, reflected pool or sea light, sunset backlight, or blue hour. Prefer side-backlight, rim light, or reflected water light when natural.

Use asymmetric framing. Permit one or two modest photographic imperfections:

- subject slightly off-center;
- a forearm or hair tip clipped by the frame;
- foreground foliage, spray, or droplets partially obscuring the view;
- a slight horizon tilt;
- small motion blur or autofocus lag;
- localized highlight clipping, flare, bloom, or soft corners.

Treat these as camera mistakes, never anatomical or generative mistakes.

For skin, favor pores, fine facial hair, mild uneven tone, lip texture, under-eye detail, subtle flushing, faint freckles or tan marks, and restrained sweat/water sheen. Avoid airbrushed uniformity.

## 8. Failure prevention

Reject or revise any concept that implies:

- collage, contact sheet, grid, multiple panels, storyboard, film-strip layout, nested photographs, or multiple times/places;
- underage or childlike swimwear depiction;
- visible nipples, areolae, or genitals;
- full battle gear used unchanged in water when a swimwear translation is intended;
- all people standing evenly, facing front, and looking at the lens;
- unrelated actions with no shared event;
- face fusion, identity swaps, repeated people, character-attribute leakage, or identical outfits with only color changes;
- plastic skin, CGI skin, waxy texture, implausible anatomy, extra limbs/fingers, fused bodies, or extreme proportions;
- a camera-screen UI, REC mark, timestamp, focus box, logo, or watermark.

The final impression should be: they were already spending the summer together, and the camera happened to catch this instant.
